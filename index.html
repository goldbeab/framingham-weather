The Weather Story

A warm front is lifting through southern New England tonight, bringing widespread steady rain and unusually mild temperatures. Expect lows to bottom out in the mid-to-upper 40s around midnight before temperatures actually rise overnight as warmer air advects in from the south. Dewpoints will climb into the low-to-mid 50s, creating muggy conditions that are quite unusual for late November.

Patchy fog may develop overnight, though surface winds should be strong enough to prevent any dense or widespread fog formation. Plan on 0.25 to 0.50 inches of rainfall through the overnight hours.

Wednesday morning brings improving conditions as a mid-level dry slot suppresses widespread precipitation. Expect isolated to scattered light showers through the day, but many hours will be dry. The headline for Wednesday is the above-normal temperatures—highs reaching the upper 50s to low 60s, running 10-15 degrees above the seasonal average.

The pattern shifts dramatically Wednesday night as a strong cold front pushes through the region. Behind it, temperatures will plummet with 850 hPa temperatures dropping to -5°C. This translates to blustery, much colder conditions for Thanksgiving. The good news? Thanksgiving looks dry with mostly sunny skies—perfect for holiday gatherings and outdoor activities, though you'll want to bundle up.

Friday brings the season's coldest air yet with widespread gusts reaching 35-45 mph, especially in higher terrain. The coldest morning will likely be Saturday or Sunday if mid and high clouds hold off until near sunrise. Looking ahead, a moderating trend returns by Sunday with another frontal boundary approaching early next week.

Key Takeaways

  • Dry, sunny Thanksgiving—perfect for holiday plans, just bundle up for 45°F and gusty winds

Bottom Line: Get through tonight's rain and you'll be rewarded with a dry, sunny Thanksgiving—just trade the umbrella for a warm coat as winter-like cold and blustery winds take hold through the holiday weekend.
